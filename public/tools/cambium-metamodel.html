Cambium

A metamodel of organizational flow

This models an organization in motion: what it must always be able to do, who is arranged against that today, what it runs on, and — the part most models leave out — how a decision is made, who may act on it without asking, and what anybody has actually gone and measured.

Twenty-two entity types. Small enough to hold in your head, because a notation you can hold in your head is one people keep current, and a model nobody updates is worse than no model at all.

The entities

Twenty-two, grouped by how long they last. The grouping is the first thing the model asserts: what an organization can do outlives how it is arranged to do it, and a model that mixes the two goes stale at every reorganization.

Durable Still true after replacing every system and reorganizing every team.
Legal entitycountry · form · parent
The company that signs things. A boundary no mandate crosses: a decision binding one entity does not bind another, and the route between two runs through a contract rather than a conversation.
Capabilityoutcome · core|supporting|generic · max 4 levels
Something the organization must always be able to do — not what it happens to be doing this year. It answers one question, and the answer has to survive replacing the system, the team and the vendor currently behind it. The classification is what makes every finding readable: neglect in a core capability is the business losing its advantage, and the same neglect in a generic one usually means it should have been bought years ago.
Bounded contextowner · classification
The boundary inside which a word means one thing. It is where a local language lives, and it outlives the team that speaks it.
Information objectagreed · a reading per context
A business concept, plus what each context calls it and takes it to mean. Two teams can hold identical definitions and still misunderstand each other, so the local term is recorded beside the local meaning.
Offeringkind · audience · stage
What the organization actually hands somebody. Everything else in the model is an input, and an organization can have all its inputs in order and still be unable to say what it delivers.
Customerwho receives it
Named rather than implied, because “the customer” is the word most likely to mean four things.
Flowsteps · value per month · returns to
A piece of work end to end, naming the capabilities it passes through. Deliberately thin — a sequence changes constantly and a capability does not. Where it returns to is nullable, and null is the finding.
Arranged True this year. Expected to change, and the model expects it.
Organizational unitmandate: business · technical · spend
A team, and what it may settle without asking. Mandate rather than ownership is what decides whether work moves or travels, and it is never visible on an org chart.
Actorperson | agent | agent team · runs on · accountable to
Whoever does the work, which no longer means a person. An agent holds competence, sits in a team and counts toward the load — and cannot sign, because a signature records that somebody would stand behind a sentence, and that cannot be delegated to something a vendor can switch off. Every agent answers to one named human being: responsibility divides between people, accountability does not.
Competenceheld by · staffs
What somebody can do, separate from what they are employed as. Capability belongs to the organization; competence belongs to people, and people resign.
Decision gateauthority · cadence
Where work waits for somebody. A fortnightly gate on a daily flow is not a policy, it is thirteen days of waiting, and the cadence is what makes that arithmetic rather than opinion.
Realized What the durable half is currently made of.
Systemvendor · cost · phase · CIA
A system realizes a capability and is not one. Replacing it should not change what the organization can do.
Interfacepattern · rung · queue · fields
How something is reached. The pattern decides what a change costs far more than sync-or-async does: an API can be versioned, an event replayed, a nightly file cannot be changed without finding everybody who parses it, and a shared database cannot be changed at all. The queue behind it is drawn, because to whoever is standing in it that is not an implementation detail.
Infrastructureprovider · region · environment
Where things actually run. Coarse on purpose — the moment it holds every host it is a second inventory disagreeing with the real one. What earns a place is something whose failure would be an organizational event.
Technologyring · suited to · unsuited to
What you build with, including how you work — practices and techniques sit on the same radar as languages. A ring says whether something new may start here; suitability says what for, and the two disagreeing is where a person decides.
Exitwhat · how · cost · last proved
How you would leave. The date is the field that matters: a migration nobody has rehearsed is a hope, and the ones that do not work turn out not to work on the day you need them.
Movement None of this can be read off a repository. All of it is why the rest moves or does not.
Agreementparties · signatures · bears
A statement about one thing, and who has put their name to it. The signature is the only honest evidence something is shared rather than asserted — and `bears` names who lives with the consequence as distinct from who signed, because decided upstairs and felt downstairs is the ordinary shape of an organizational decision.
Guidelinelevel · autonomy · check · derogates · review
A rule about a class of things, signed once and then checked against everything new — the signature that scales. Levels let a narrow rule override a broad one, and it must name the one it displaces: declared, that is an exception made on purpose; undeclared, it is a contradiction somebody discovers at the worst moment.
Decisionproduces · settles · needs · affects
What was decided, and what it changed. Recording one applies it: the rule it announces becomes a guideline, the meaning becomes an agreement, the competence attaches to the capability that needs it. Everything it creates arrives unsigned — a decision is somebody choosing, a rule is the organization agreeing to be held to it.
Askkind · advancement · options · became a rule
An actor stopped and needs a person. Carries what it already worked out and which option it would take, because “what should I do” wastes the reader and “I would do B, tell me if that is wrong” costs a minute. An answer promoted to a rule is asked once; one that is not gets asked again next week in different words.
Observationindicator · reading · who looked
What somebody went and measured, against one of four indicators — ownership clarity, handoff loss, decision latency, learning cycle time. Append-only, and never summed.
Assessmentrisk · compliance · security · continuity
The answers a repository cannot give: whether an outage is a regulatory event, whether the data is somebody’s medical history, whether anyone outside the team has ever reviewed it. Unknown is a first-class answer — a compliance field defaulting to “none” is a claim nobody made.
Overridewhy · until · raised to
Going against a rule you signed, on purpose, in writing. Possible, cheap, and impossible without saying why — because forbidding it means it happens quietly, and a rule broken quietly teaches an organization that its rules are theatre.
Activitycommit · deploy · run · incident
What was actually done. The only thing here that cannot be derived — it has to be written back by whatever did it, and until something does, a team’s record shows what was decided rather than what happened.

What the model asserts

Six rules. Each is a thing the shape of the model makes true, rather than advice attached to it.

What you can do outlives how you are arranged to do it

Capabilities, contexts and meanings are durable; teams and systems are this decade’s answer. Keeping them in separate bands is what stops the map going stale at every reorganization — and makes “who owns this” a relationship rather than a property of the thing.

Nothing is shared until somebody signs it

Every other model gives each entity one owner by construction and so cannot express two groups both claiming something, which is the commonest real finding there is. Here a statement carries its parties, and an unsigned one stays visibly unsigned.

Autonomy is derived, never set

What an actor may do comes from the guidelines in force where it is working: act where a signed rule already decided the matter, prepare and stop where judgement remains, ask where no rule reaches. There is no switch. Widening what agents may do and deciding more things out loud are therefore the same act.

Accountability does not divide

A team can be responsible for a capability — several people carry it between them and any of them can pick up the question. A thing that acts on its own needs one named person, because when it produces something wrong the question is not who carries this but who explains it and who decides to stop it.

Structure is read; movement is observed

The first three bands can be derived from what an organization already keeps, which is why the model produces findings in its first hour. The fourth cannot be derived at all, and a model with no place for it quietly asserts that structure is sufficient.

Nothing is summed

The four indicators are reported separately and never rolled into a score, and neither are risk levels, lock-in reasons or delivery figures. A single organizational number acquires a target, a target acquires a bonus, and a measure with a bonus attached stops measuring anything.

The model is YAML in git, not a database. Diffs, history for free, code review on organizational change, and a map that survives the tool that drew it.

Which is also the last thing it asserts: a metamodel is only worth having if the organization it describes can argue with it in the open, one commit at a time.
