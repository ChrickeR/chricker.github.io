# TROi — Organizational Flow

The source of [troi.se](https://troi.se), and of the paper it exists to publish.

**Organizational Flow** is a working theory of how organizations create value, published a chapter at a time at [troi.se/organizational-flow](https://troi.se/organizational-flow/). It argues one thing:

> Organizations don't become slow because people work slowly. They become slow because structural friction accumulates faster than value is created.

And it names the property that decides whether they move at all:

> Organizational Flow rises as information, decisions, execution and learning move through enduring capabilities with clear ownership — and falls as structural friction accumulates faster than value is created.

The contribution is not any of the individual parts — capabilities come out of enterprise architecture, psychological safety is Amy Edmondson's, living systems have a lineage running back decades, and all of it is credited where it appears. It is the argument connecting them: that architecture, capabilities, ownership, leadership and culture are not separate disciplines but different descriptions of the same organizational system, and that architecture's real job is reducing the structural friction in that system rather than designing technology or org charts.

Christoffer Råsten · independent architecture and organizational design · <christoffer.rasten@troi.se>

## What's here

The manuscript, not just a site to put it on.

- **`src/content/chapters/`** — 56 chapters across four parts (6 · 28 · 16 · 6), plus the Preface and three pieces of front matter, at around 61,000 words. Part II observes what friction looks like from inside an organization and never prescribes; Part III is where the cultivating happens, and most of its chapters point back at the observation they answer. Markdown carries a title, a dek, the plain subject noun, which indicator and half it works on, and optional Editor's Notes; nothing else.
- **`src/data/book-structure.ts`** — the single source of truth for the paper's shape: parts, front matter, appendix, reading order, chapter numbering. Chapters don't know where they live; this file decides, so reordering never means touching a chapter.
- **`src/content/insights/`** — seventeen short essays that appear in the margins of the chapters they belong to.
- **`src/content/blog/`** — the archive this paper grew out of, migrated with its original URLs intact.
- **`src/data/acknowledgments.ts`, `editors-note.ts`, `reader-note.ts`, `self-assessment.ts`** — content shared by the web, print and download editions, so the three can't drift apart.
- **`public/audio/`** — the whole paper narrated, about 5h 30m across 59 files, generated by `scripts/generate-audio.py` with Microsoft's neural voices. Regenerating after a rewrite needs `--force`; the script skips any chapter whose file already exists.

The paper is published in three forms from one source: the chapter pages, narration on every chapter and on the whole thing, and a [Print Edition](https://troi.se/organizational-flow/print/) set as a 135 × 215 mm book — cover, front matter, mirrored margins, back cover — which is also what a physical copy is printed from. `docs/print-edition.md` covers the book: what the browser can produce today, what a real print run still needs, and the flags that hide the PDF and the edition itself.

Three things exist because a paper this long is itself friction. [The Ten-Minute Version](https://troi.se/organizational-flow/the-ten-minute-version/) is the whole argument standing on its own, and [a Swedish translation of it](https://troi.se/organizational-flow/sv/) sits beside it. [A Closer Look](https://troi.se/organizational-flow/self-assessment/) asks eighteen questions about what you can observe where you work and then points at the chapters that match — it reports what the answers seem to suggest rather than scoring anything, and the answers never leave the browser: they live in the link you get at the end, not on a server.

## Working on it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # localhost:4321
npm run check    # pre-publish report
npm run build    # runs check first — a failed check fails the build
```

`scripts/check.mjs` is a gate rather than a linter. It verifies that every chapter is placed in the structure and every structure entry has a file, that titles match, that internal links resolve, that headings don't skip levels, that Insights point at chapters which exist, and that terminology and spelling stay consistent across chapters and Insights. It also rejects numeric chapter cross-references, because chapter numbers shift the moment one is added. It has caught three real regressions that would otherwise have shipped.

One thing the gate does *not* yet cover: whether a chapter's narration is older than the chapter. Audio has gone out of step four separate ways — a regeneration that skipped every existing file and looked like success, a reading order mirrored by hand that dropped two chapters from the player, a regeneration that finished after its own commit, and a generator process left running long enough to overwrite good narration with text that had since been rewritten. Until that check exists, `--force` and a timestamp comparison are done by hand.

Astro, static output, no client framework. Deployed to GitHub Pages by `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` on push to `master`. The build artifact is around 100 MB and almost all of it is audio, so the deploy step carries a raised timeout.
