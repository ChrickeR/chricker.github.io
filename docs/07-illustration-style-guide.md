# Illustration Style Guide — Project Canopy, Phase 3

No illustration exists yet — Phase 2 pressure-tested text and layout only. This doc is a proposal to review before any art gets commissioned or drawn, not a record of decisions already tested.

## The four families, and where each belongs

Per the brief, all imagery routes through one forest ecosystem, split into four families that must never blur into a fifth:

| Family | Represents | Primary home |
|---|---|---|
| **The Root System** | Capabilities | Part II — already referenced in prose in the Capabilities chapter ("capabilities are the root system") |
| **The Garden** | Culture, care over time | Part I — Gardening Organizations |
| **The Canopy** | Value created, what the outside world experiences | Site-wide brand imagery, hero/OG contexts — the canopy is the "face" of the whole book |
| **The Seasons** | Change, learning cycles | Part IV — Applying Organizational Flow |

Part III (Designing Organizations) doesn't get a dedicated family — its chapters (Leadership, Architecture, Technology, AI, Friction) are the most abstract/structural in the book and are better served by Root System or Seasons imagery reused with a different crop or moment, rather than inventing a fifth metaphor to fill the gap. Reuse before invention.

## Medium

No technical diagrams, no UML/Mermaid/Archimate/BPMN (explicit brief constraint) — and no photography or 3D render either, since both would break the "beautifully published book" register the typography already establishes. **Recommendation: single-color ink-line illustration**, drawn in one of the palette's accent tones (moss, bark, or gold) on the paper background — closer to a woodcut or botanical-plate line drawing than a modern flat-vector icon set. This keeps illustration in the same register as the type: warm, restrained, printed-book-like, and — critically — it ages the same way the typography does, rather than looking like a 2026 web illustration trend in five years.

## Where illustration appears

- **Part openers only**, not every chapter. Twenty-one chapter-header illustrations is a production commitment the book doesn't need to make on day one, and repetition of the same four families across twenty-one chapters would dilute rather than reinforce them. Four illustrations (one per Part, using that Part's family) covers the whole book structurally.
- **Model pages get one illustration each** — this is where illustration is load-bearing, not decorative, since a Model *is* the visual metaphor. Every Model page needs its family's artwork, reframed for that specific model's idea.
- **Insights, Blog, About, Contact stay illustration-free.** Consistent with the Component Library's "no cards, no boxes" restraint — illustration marks structural moments (entering a Part, encountering a Model), not every page.

## Placement and sizing

Large and singular, never inline mid-paragraph — reading rhythm shouldn't break for a decorative aside. Part-opener art sits above the kicker, full reading-column width or wider; Model-page art sits at the top of the page, above the H1, same treatment as a large hero image would get. No captions unless the illustration needs one to make sense on its own (most won't).

## Motion

Static only. The one animation already in the design system (`docs/03-design-system.md`) is the hero's restrained rise-in fade — illustration should not introduce a second, competing animation language. If a Model illustration ever needs to show a process (e.g., seasons cycling), express it as a single static image implying motion (the way a seed-to-canopy botanical plate implies growth without frames), not as an animated or scroll-triggered sequence.

## Open question

None of this has been tested with an actual artist or generated image — recommend commissioning or drafting one Part-opener (suggest: Part II, Root System, since Capabilities is the only chapter with real content to illustrate against) before committing to ink-line as the final medium.
