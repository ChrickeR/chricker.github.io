# Principles Reference — Organizational Flow

Full principle taxonomy supplied by the author, grouped by chapter/theme, to guide writing across the whole book — not just the chapters already live. Where a principle is already represented in a published chapter, that's noted. Where it isn't, it's a candidate for the chapter once written.

## The Twelve (author's own shortlist — "The Twelve Principles of Organizational Flow")

1. Purpose gives direction. — *Purpose (published, covered in substance, not verbatim)*
2. Technology is rarely the bottleneck. Friction is. — *Organizational Principles (published, verbatim)*
3. Flow over friction. — *Organizational Principles (published, added as closing line)*
4. Ownership before governance. — *Ownership + Organizational Principles (published, verbatim)*
5. Contribution creates flow. — *People (published, added)*
6. Information should move. — *Organizational Principles (published, covered)*
7. Decisions create momentum. — *Organizational Principles (published, covered as "decisions create flow")*
8. Execution is where strategy learns. — *Organizational Principles (published, verbatim)*
9. Learning compounds. — *Organizational Principles (published, covered as "design for learning")*
10. Capabilities endure. — *Capabilities + Organizational Principles (published, verbatim)*
11. People own. AI amplifies. Organizations learn. — *People + Organizational Principles (published, verbatim)*
12. Architecture should make organizations easier to change. — *Organizational Principles (published, verbatim)*

## Full taxonomy by theme

**Purpose** — Purpose gives direction. *(Purpose, published)*

**Flow** — Technology is rarely the bottleneck, friction is; flow over friction; flow is an organizational capability; flow has a human dependency. *(Organizational Principles + new "Flow has a Human Dependency" Insight, published)*

**Ownership** — Ownership before governance; governance grows where ownership is unclear; ownership isn't about control, it's about accountability; clear ownership predicts adaptability. *(Ownership, published, verbatim)*

**People** — Contribution creates flow; people create the conditions for each other's success; trust grows when people consistently help others move; organizations are different, interdependence isn't. *(People, published)*

**AI** — People own, AI amplifies, organizations learn; AI should amplify capability not replace accountability; the question isn't what AI can do, it's who owns the outcome. *(People, published — will also anchor the future Part III "Artificial Intelligence" chapter)*

**Information** — Information should move; creates value when shared; captured once, used many times; copying and reconciling creates friction. *(Organizational Principles touches this; full chapter not yet written — Part II "Information")*

**Decisions** — Decisions create momentum; slow decisions slow organizations; a good decision made early beats a perfect decision made too late. *(Organizational Principles touches this; full chapter not yet written — Part II "Decisions")*

**Execution** — Execution is where strategy learns; strategy is a hypothesis until executed; execution creates learning. *(Organizational Principles touches this; full chapter not yet written — Part II "Execution")*

**Learning** — Experience is automatic, learning is a choice; organizations learn through execution; learning compounds over time. *(Organizational Principles touches this; full chapter not yet written — Part II "Learning")*

**Capabilities** — Capabilities endure; technology enables capabilities, doesn't define them; organizations create value through capabilities, not technology. *(Capabilities, published)*

**Architecture** — Architecture should make organizations easier to change; creates the conditions for adaptation; exists to improve flow; technology/process/architecture cannot compensate for unclear ownership. *(Organizational Principles touches this; full chapter not yet written — Part III "Architecture")*

**Leadership** — Leaders create conditions, people create flow; expectations create responsibility; leadership is cultivating, not controlling. *(Not yet written — Part III "Leadership")*

**Organizational Design** — Cultivate, don't control; organizations should be designed to learn; organizations are gardens, not machines; change is a capability, not a project. *(Gardening Organizations + Organizational Principles cover "cultivate not control" and "gardens not machines" at length; "change is a capability, not a project" added to Organizational Principles; the rest is scattered across Part III as those chapters get written — there's no single "Organizational Design" chapter in the current structure, since Part III itself is titled "Designing Organizations.")*
