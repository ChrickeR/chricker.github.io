# Editorial Guidelines — Project Canopy, Phase 3

Basis: the voice notes in [Content Strategy](02-content-strategy-and-mapping.md), pressure-tested by actually writing the Preface and the Capabilities chapter in Phase 2. Where those two chapters made a judgment call, it's written down here so the next seventeen chapters make the same one.

## Register

Target the 2026 posts' register, not the 2021–2022 or 2025 register. The corpus tightens over time — 2021–2022 is hedged and explanatory, the 2025 batch is framework-listy and consultant-toned, 2026 is terse and aphoristic. The Capabilities chapter (written in Phase 2, sourced from a 2025 post) was deliberately pulled toward the 2026 register in the rewrite — shorter sentences, fewer qualifiers, a one-line closer. Keep pulling every curated chapter the same direction, regardless of which year its source post came from.

## First person

Use "I" for: the Preface, Part I chapters (they're explicitly a personal journey), and anecdotes anywhere else in the book (the card-game story in Capabilities is the model for this — first-person for the specific memory, third-person for the general claim around it). Use plain declarative voice — "organizations," "teams," not "I think organizations" — for the conceptual core of Part II–IV chapters. Don't retrofit "I" into a chapter that doesn't need it just for warmth; the corpus audit found only one genuinely first-person post in five years, and manufacturing false intimacy everywhere would read as try-hard against that baseline.

## Metaphor discipline

Root System / Garden / Canopy / Seasons — no fifth family, ever. The Capabilities chapter uses Root System once ("capabilities are the root system") and leaves it there rather than extending the metaphor sentence after sentence — a single, well-placed anchor lands harder than a paragraph that tries to make everything about roots. Supporting imagery from the source posts (cartography, in Capabilities: "closer to cartography than documentation") can stay as a minor aside as long as it doesn't compete with the forest family for the sentence doing the real work. The excluded metaphors from the brief — River, Capability Tree, Bridge, Ecosystem, Mountain Pass — don't get partial credit either; "bridge between business and technology" from the original Capabilities post was deliberately cut in the rewrite for exactly this reason.

## Terminology

- **Organizational Flow** — always spelled out, never abbreviated to "OF."
- **capability**, not *capacity* — the two get conflated in casual writing; capability is the correct term throughout.
- **Insight**, **Model**, **Reference**, **Chapter** — capitalize only when naming the content type itself ("this became an Insight," not "an insight into the problem").
- **the Living Book** — capitalized as a proper noun when referring to Organizational Flow as a whole, same register as "the blog."

## Citation style

Name sources in prose, the way "First Principle" cites Kry, Jan Boklöv, and David Epstein — no footnotes, no reference-list apparatus inside a chapter. That belongs to the back-matter References page, which is the one place a more structured list is appropriate. In-chapter citation should read like a book crediting an idea, not a paper citing a source.

## The forward-link line

Exact template, used only on the original archived blog post, only when its idea is developed further in the book:

> *This idea is developed further in [chapter link].*

Always italic, always this exact wording, always one line at the end of the post, never a paragraph explaining the connection. The archive stays untouched otherwise — this is the one line it's allowed to gain.

## Length

Insights: 2–5 minutes, roughly 400–700 words — the "Capability before Technology" Insight (Phase 2) landed at ~350 words, on the short end but inside range; don't pad it. Chapters: no hard cap, but if a chapter starts accumulating numbered steps or a benefits list (the shape "OKR," "DORA metrics," and "Tech Radar" took in the source corpus), that's the signal it belongs on the References page instead, not evidence it needs trimming into a chapter. A chapter argues; a Reference explains a framework. Don't let one drift into the other's shape.
