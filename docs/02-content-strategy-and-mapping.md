## Coverage gaps — where new writing is required

The book has 21 chapters across four parts. Only 3 have a direct source post. The rest need original writing, curated from the recurring themes below rather than migrated from a single article:

- **Part I:** "A Personal Journey" and "Organizations as Systems" have no direct source. Open question — does "A Personal Story of Digital Awakening" actually cover both "A Personal Journey" and "A Digital Awakening," or should it be split into two chapters? Read as one continuous narrative today.
- **Part II:** "Information" has no source material anywhere in the corpus — full new chapter.
- **Part III:** "Leadership" and "Friction" have no dedicated posts.
- **Part IV — thinnest part overall.** Only fragments touch its four chapters (Finding the Right Angle → Observing Organizations; First Principle → Questions Worth Asking; Embarking on a Journey → Where to Start), and none squarely. "Improving Flow" has nothing. Budget the most net-new writing time here.

## Recurring themes across the corpus

One claim restated across five years: *technology is a means, purpose/capability is the end, clarity of ownership beats control.* Guardrails, bounded contexts, capability mapping, and "structured autonomy" are the same idea recurring in 2021, 2022, and 2025. A second thread is anti-monolith / anti-centralization thinking. A third, newest thread (2026) applies the same ownership/purpose lens to AI and AI-assisted coding — the corpus is already walking toward Part III's AI chapter in real time, which is a good sign the book's spine fits the actual body of thought.

## Voice notes (for editorial guidelines, Phase 3)

- Frequent one-line aphoristic closers: *"Value is the goal." "Stop building bigger systems. Start building smarter boundaries." "The real problem is where the logic ended up."*
- Heavy use of concrete metaphor to make architecture tangible: photography, soccer, gardening, cartography, radar. The book should keep this instinct but funnel new metaphors through the one forest family (Root System / Garden / Canopy / Seasons) rather than introducing a new one per chapter.
- Sentence rhythm has tightened over time — 2021–2022 posts are more hedged and explanatory; 2026 posts are close to telegraphic. Editorial guidelines should pick a target point on that spectrum rather than defaulting to "however the source post was written."
- Almost everything is written in an abstract second/third person ("teams," "organizations"). Only the Digital Awakening post is first-person. If Part I is meant to read as a personal journey, more first-person material will likely need to be written, not excerpted.
