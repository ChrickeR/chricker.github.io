# Voice — pre-publish checklist

Reference posts for this voice: [Cheap Code Gets Expensive Fast](src/content/blog/cheap-code-gets-expensive-fast.md), [Where Logic Ends Up Matters](src/content/blog/where-logic-ends-up-matters.md), the [Preface](src/content/chapters/preface.md).

## 1. The headline owns a claim, not a topic

A topic invites a reader to guess what you think. A claim tells them.

- Bad: *What Digital Transformation Entails and Why It Matters*
- Good: *Where Logic Ends Up Matters*
- Bad: *Attracting Talent*
- Good: *Cheap Code Gets Expensive Fast*

Test: read the headline alone. If it could introduce three different arguments, it's a topic. Rewrite until only one argument fits.

## 2. No generic consulting term without earning it first

"Digital transformation," "strategic alignment," "talent," "capability" — these are allowed, but only after the sentence has done the work of saying something specific. A term earns its place when a concrete claim precedes it; it doesn't earn its place when it's standing in for one.

- Bad: *"Digital transformation is one of those terms that's everywhere."* (opens on the term itself)
- Good: *"Misplaced logic rarely causes problems immediately."* (opens on a specific, checkable claim — the vocabulary shows up later, already earned)

Test: could this sentence appear in any consulting deck, unchanged? If yes, cut it or make it specific to this argument.

## 3. Short sentences. Restrained tone. No buzzword lists.

Fragments are allowed and often stronger than complete sentences. A one-line paragraph is a legitimate unit, not a formatting accident.

- *"That doesn't make it cheap."*
- *"Not the rest."*
- *"Everything works."*

Avoid stacking adjectives or listing benefits ("clearer, faster, more aligned, more transparent"). One claim per sentence. If a sentence has two ideas, it's two sentences.

## 4. Concrete image over abstract claim

Prefer a specific, physical, or narrative image to a general statement, when one is available.

- Bad: *"Executives play a critical role by setting a clear digital vision."*
- Good: *"A small rule here. A convenient check there. Everything works."* — shows the drift instead of asserting that drift happens.

If the only way to make a point is a bare abstraction ("architecture should make organizations easier to change"), that's fine — but check first whether a concrete case, image, or moment would say it better.

## 5. Own the ending

The last line is a claim, not a summary and not a call to action ("So, take the first step — align on meaning, keep learning"). It should be quotable on its own, out of context.

- *"Cheap code gets expensive fast."*
- *"The real problem is where the logic ended up."*

## Before publishing, check:

- [ ] Headline is a claim, not a topic
- [ ] No consulting term appears before it's earned
- [ ] No sentence has two ideas in it
- [ ] At least one concrete image, not just abstract claims
- [ ] Last line is a standalone claim
